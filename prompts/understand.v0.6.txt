QUANTITATIVE COVERAGE AND CLAIM-SCOPED EVIDENCE — policy 0.6

Reading coverage is not quantitative extraction coverage. Build quantitative_coverage from the actual main/SI figures and tables, not from the observations you happened to record. Inventory every relevant final-material performance panel and each distinguishable curve/condition/cycle series. Keep necessary controls when they support design comparisons; do not let precursors or characterization displace the final system. Give other scientific panels a disposition and scope, including mechanism and external_benchmark. Reference tables' other-paper rows are not this study's experiments.

Each coverage entry has a stable id, canonical source_id/block_id, locator (figure/panel/table/row as applicable), label, data_kind, scope, state, expected_points (only if count can actually be established), observation_ids, reason and limitations. Separate curves with different material, oxidant, pollutant, concentration, pH, co-solute, cycle or storage state. An endpoint-only extraction of a multi-point curve is partial, never complete. An inspected image can be read_structured in reading coverage while its quantitative entry remains partial/unquantified/unreadable. No inventory, unknown expected counts or model agreement proves completeness.

For readable final-system time courses, condition series, cycle series, fresh-versus-aged comparisons and outdoor time series, capture the visible author-sampled points and directly printed values, rather than only maxima, endpoints, ranges or prose notes. Reuse existing observation IDs for unchanged facts. Set observation.series_id to the matching coverage id and coordinates to NamedFact entries (for example elapsed_time, cycle_index, storage_duration, clock_time, pH); retain units, basis/reference and evidence. Preserve original experimental grouping and time origin, including adsorption versus illuminated reaction. Do not create duplicate experiments merely to represent time points. Never infer hidden points, interpolate to fill gaps, invent an expected point count, fabricate individual cycles from an average, or treat a smooth fitted line as raw experimental samples. If readable points remain omitted, distinguish a missing major result from optional enrichment of a faithfully scoped partial series. Wrong or unsupported assertions, incorrect complete/not_reported claims, missing major final-system results, and missing observations that could materially change the conclusion require blocking revision. Do not use partial as an exemption for a missing major outcome, failure event, principal comparison or necessary experimental interpretation. When retained major results are supported and only additional readable interior points remain, preserve partial with its exact reason and limitations. In ReadingAudit, record this additional work in typed quantitative_followups rather than needs_revision or potential_omissions. This is not a claim of complete digitization: restrict the named comparisons/recalculations to supported recorded points. Resolution, overlap, unavailable raw data and bounded acquisition remain distinct limitations; never fill hidden points or invent a count. Output structures without a quantitative_followups field should retain a scoped limitation instead of adding unsupported schema fields. Missing raw data is not a zero.

Numerical provenance has independent aspects. A number explicitly printed in prose/table/label may have value.status=reported; origin still distinguishes reported_measurement, author_calculation or author_simulation. A numerical reading from axes, a bar, a curve or a heatmap has value.status=estimated. Ordinarily use origin=plot_estimate, but retain origin=author_simulation for estimated simulation output and describe the visual estimation in value.raw/note and limitations. Record only defensible precision; an overlapping near-zero bar is not an exact zero. Keep digitization uncertainty distinct from the authors' experimental error bars. Do not relabel a plot estimate as author-reported because the underlying paper reported the plot. If a later table gives the same value explicitly, identify that exact same material/condition/metric before changing provenance.

Provenance applies to coordinates and experimental metadata as well as the dependent numerical value. A time or other coordinate read from a marker between labeled ticks is estimated unless an explicit sampling schedule or value supports that exact coordinate; retain horizontal digitization uncertainty separately. Do not label an entire compound statement reported when it mixes direct source information with inherited or inferred protocol details: split the claims or explicitly qualify the uncertain part with the appropriate Fact status. Before assigning a shared method to a specific experiment, establish from the paper that the method applies to that group; otherwise preserve inferred/unresolved applicability and the affected comparison limitation. An explicit generally applicable protocol can support its stated groups, but nearby methods or a shared material name alone cannot establish applicability. These checks also apply to substance formulas, material classifications, energy conditions and time-window references; correct their exact source locations without inventing missing information.

Evidence must support the particular claim, not merely mention the same material or appear nearby. For numeric observations, inspect EACH value.evidence_ids entry against the exact number/axis, material or sample, experimental group, metric, time and table row/figure panel. A benchmark table row for the final material cannot support control-material values or a different metric not present in that row. An evaporation plot cannot directly support a pollutant-degradation percentage. Keep directly supporting references in value.evidence_ids, and provide evidence_links with evidence_id, supported_fields (observation-relative field names such as value, unit, material_id, experiment_id, time_window), support_type (direct/context/conflict), and explanation. Context/method evidence may remain observation-level but must not be presented as direct numerical support. Check evidence already present as well as adding missing evidence. An ID or text match is not semantic support.

Keep signed change and positive loss distinct: evaporation_mass_change means the signed change m(t)-m(0); evaporation_mass_loss is a positive loss only when that convention is established. Preserve raw signed values and source axis wording. Any sign conversion belongs in normalized with normalization_reason and an explicit metric convention, not a silent absolute value. Unit conversions, concentration normalization and recalculated rate constants likewise require a documented basis; endpoint removal does not establish a kinetic fitting model or window. Author-reported k and a separately recalculated k are different observations.

Mechanistic pathways stated or proposed by the authors are author_hypothesis, not the extraction model's model_inference and not established reactions merely because arrows are drawn. Preserve proposed reactant/product/path edges in named, versioned extension fields with evidence where the supported schema allows, or explicitly record the schema/identity limitation. Molecular m/z alone does not identify a unique structure. For toxicity predictions, preserve author_simulation, endpoint/species, compound identity and log/linear scale. A color-only heatmap does not provide exact numerical cells; record qualitative classes or justified estimates with uncertainty, or mark unquantified. Do not fabricate exact numbers for every cell.

Unreported reaction volume, illuminated area, collected condensate yield, condensate concentration, mineralization and other conditions remain not_reported only after checking available sources; unread, unavailable, unquantified and conflict stay distinct. Do not turn acquisition or extraction incompleteness into author-not-reported. Quantitative limitations restrict affected kinetic/condition/cycle comparisons and recomputation while preserving supported paper/material retrieval.
